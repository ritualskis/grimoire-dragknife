Understanding Drag Knife Blade Offset & Corner Swivel

1

Why Plain G-Code Fails with Drag Knives

Unlike a rotating endmill that cuts equally in all directions around its center, a drag knife blade acts like a caster wheel on an office chair or razor blade swivel.

The razor edge lags behind the tool spindle center by a fixed distance e called the Blade Offset. If your CNC runs standard line coordinates, sharp corners turn into rounded fillet tears and ragged leaves.

2

The Corner Swivel Action

To cut a sharp 90° or acute corner:

  1. The machine moves past the nominal sharp point by offset length e so the trailing blade edge hits the corner vertex point $(X_c, Y_c)$.
  2. The tool stops progressing down the line, and executes a circular arc (G2 or G3) centered on $(X_c, Y_c)$ with radius e.
  3. During this arc, the razor tip remains planted stationary in the corner point while the tool spindle center swings around it to face the new heading!
